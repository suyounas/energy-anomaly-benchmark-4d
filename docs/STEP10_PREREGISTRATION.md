# Step 10 pre-registration: do detector rankings on real energy data hold across many independent events?

Written and committed before any Step 10 detector is run. The series list, the event list, the event-to-series mapping and the analysis below are fixed by this document and `docs/step10_events.csv`. Nothing is changed after seeing results; deviations go in `docs/STEP10_RESULT.md`.

## Question
The real-data comparison (Step 8) has two events per series, which cannot rank detectors. Step 10 repeats the same equal-budget comparison on many balancing-authority (BA) series and on many independently dated events, so that (a) the ranking has a usable sample and (b) the conditional behaviour found in the factorial design (Step 9) can be checked on observed data.

## Series (selection rule, fixed in advance)
* Source: EIA Hourly Electric Grid Monitor (EIA-930), demand (`D`), demand-adjusted series where available, hourly.
* Include every balancing authority that has, for 2019-07-01 to 2026-07-31, at least 95% non-missing hourly demand and no gap longer than 72 hours after the standard EIA-930 cleaning already used in the ERCOT pipeline. BAs are not chosen by looking at detector output.
* The qualifying list is written to `results/step10/series_manifest.csv` (BA code, coverage, gaps) before any detector is run, and every qualifying BA is analysed.

## Events (selection rule, fixed in advance)
Two source classes, always reported separately and then pooled:
* **Class A, operator or regulator declared.** Events from grid-operator or reliability-coordinator emergency declarations (NERC Energy Emergency Alert level 2 or 3, conservation appeals, load-shed orders) and FERC/NERC event reports, entered by hand in `docs/step10_events.csv` with a source URL, dates, and a mapping rationale per balancing authority (BA).
* **Class B, weather-service derived.** Generated by `scripts/step10_select_events.py` from the NOAA/NCEI Storm Events bulk CSV (no hand selection):
  1. Event types in three families: heat (Excessive Heat, Heat); cold (Extreme Cold/Wind Chill, Winter Storm, Blizzard, Ice Storm); tropical (Hurricane, Tropical Storm).
  2. An episode covers a state in a family if at least 3 distinct zones or counties in that state have a qualifying event in the episode.
  3. A BA is affected if the states covered hold at least 50% of the BA's population, using EIA-861 balancing-authority service-territory counties and Census population (latest report year not after the event year).
  4. The window is the earliest start to the latest end of the covered states' events, as calendar dates (inclusive).
  5. Windows that overlap or are adjacent within one BA are merged. Windows of 1 to 21 calendar days are kept.
  6. `storm_group`: events whose windows overlap in time and share a family are one group (connected components), so a continent-wide wave is one cluster.
* Public holidays, scheduled outages and price spikes are not events.
* Window rule for Class A: the dates stated by the cited report; where a report gives a storm period and a core period, the storm period is primary and the core period is a pre-specified sensitivity analysis.
* The existing GB and ERCOT events are kept unchanged and reported separately; they do not count toward the Step 10 sample.
* Data window: 2019-07-01 to 2026-07-31. The end date follows a rule fixed before running: the last day of the most recent month that is at least three months older than the download date (23 Nov 2026), because NOAA enters storm records with a lag. NCEI file builds used: `StormEvents_details-ftp_v1.0_dYYYY_c20261119.csv.gz` for 2019-2026 (SHA-256 sums in `results/step10/SHA256SUMS.txt`); the 2026 file reaches 2026-08-31 and its monthly record counts were stable from January to August.
* The selection script is run once, before any detector, with the parameters above. Its output, input file hashes and summary counts are committed as `results/step10/events_selected.csv` and `results/step10/selection_summary.json`. No parameter is changed after seeing counts, except that the stated rule for fewer than 30 storm groups applies.
* Target at least 40 independent storm groups across both classes. If fewer than 30 qualify, Step 10 is reported as descriptive and no ranking claim is made.

## Detectors and protocol (unchanged from Step 8 real runs)
* The same detector set, features, equal-budget tuning, 5% alert budget, inductive fitting, five seeds and tolerance (one day) as the GB/ERCOT equal-budget runs. No new detectors are added after the runs start.
* Training and scoring (see Amendment 1): blocked temporal cross-fitting. Each BA series over the data window is split into 4 contiguous blocks of equal length. For each block, the detector is fitted on the other three blocks with every event window and a 14-day margin around it removed (segment-aware windows, as in the equal-budget runs), then scores the held-out block. Scores are rank-normalised within each block, the 5% alert budget is applied within each block, and metrics are computed on the concatenated blocks. Stochastic detectors use five seeds.

## Outcomes
* Primary: AUC-PR, and AUC-PR lift over prevalence.
* Secondary: VUS-PR (the repository's re-implementation), timestamp-level F1 at the 5% budget, per-event coverage.
* Event-level and timestamp-level metrics are reported separately and never combined.

## Primary analysis
1. Per-detector mean rank and share of events won, over all (BA, event) pairs.
2. Pairwise differences against the matrix profile and against the seasonal-naive residual, with 95% intervals from a cluster bootstrap that resamples `storm_group` clusters (events from one storm are not independent, and neither are BAs hit by the same storm).
3. Rank stability: Kendall's W across clusters, the fraction of clusters in which the overall best detector is best, and leave-one-storm-group-out agreement of the full ranking.
4. Conditional structure: ranking computed separately by event family (cold, heat, tropical) and by duration tercile, with the same cluster bootstrap.
5. Source class: every primary quantity is reported for Class A alone, Class B alone and pooled. Class A labels come from operator declarations; Class B labels say the weather was extreme, not that demand was anomalous, so they are expected to be noisier.

## Label validity (pre-specified, same as Analyses A and B)
* For every event, compute the causal seasonal-naive residual and the mean daily |z| inside the event and outside every event of the same series.
* Primary results use all events. A secondary result uses only events whose mean daily |z| exceeds the 95th percentile of daily |z| outside events for that series. This criterion uses the series and the labels only, not detector scores.
* Analysis C (label perturbations: boundary shifts of 1, 3, 7 days, dilation, drop-one-cluster) is rerun on the pooled labels.

## What each outcome would mean (no pass/fail)
* If one detector family is best in most clusters and the intervals exclude zero, a ranking claim for this set of grids and events is defensible.
* If the best detector changes with event type or duration, or no pairwise interval excludes zero, claims must be stated per event type, and the paper says so.
* Either result is reported in full.

## Limits stated in advance
Class B labels are weather-derived and state-level, so a BA is mapped by population share and not by exact footprint; the mapping may mark BAs that were barely affected, which the label-validity analysis (A and C) is there to expose; US grids only; one demand series per BA; univariate detectors; event windows from external sources are coarser than point labels; storms affect neighbouring BAs together, which the cluster bootstrap addresses but does not remove; neural detectors may be run on a subset of BAs if cost requires, and the subset is chosen by BA code order before any run and stated in the result file.

## Amendment log
**Amendment 1** (made after the event-selection run and before any detector was run).
* What the selection run produced, unchanged from the rules above: 3,444 (BA, event) pairs, 298 storm groups, 74 balancing authorities; event length median 3 days (maximum 18); events by family: heat 1,690, cold 1,507, tropical 241, heat+tropical 6; labelled share of the calendar per BA: median 0.05, maximum 0.18, none above 0.20; BAs per storm group: median 5, maximum 52.
* Problem: the original training rule (the 12 months before a BA's first event, excluding event windows) cannot be met, because a BA has about 46 events over seven years and the first event usually falls within months of the start of the data window.
* Change: the training and scoring protocol only (the cross-fitting bullet above). The series list, the event rules, the thresholds (3 zones, 50% population, 1 to 21 days), the detectors, the budget and the analysis are unchanged.
* Reason: a feasibility constraint on the design, found from the event table and not from any detector output. No detector had been run when this was written.
* The event-selection thresholds were not changed after seeing counts.
